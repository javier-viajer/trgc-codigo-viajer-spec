Global relational convergence
Version: 1.0.0 | Date: 2026-11-27
Hypotheses
Use the fixed-mass nonnegative symmetric zero-diagonal domain and fixed parameters N>=2, g_i>0, gamma>0, 0<mu<=1 of CANONICAL_LYAPUNOV_FUNCTION.md. At each iteration any admissible nonnegative unit Perron vector may be selected. A fixed point of the correspondence means a state R for which R belongs to its own update set.
Fixed-point reduction
At a fixed point R_ij=c u_i u_j off diagonal, u_i=(g_i v_i)^gamma, c>0. Active nodes S={i:v_i>0} form a complete graph of size m>=2, and all other entries vanish. The positive largest eigenvalue is simple on this graph and on the full matrix (isolated nodes contribute zero). Define T=sum u_i v_i and p_i=u_i v_i/T on S. The Perron equations give rho v_i^2=c T^2 p_i(1-p_i), so v_i^2=p_i(1-p_i)/(1-sum p_j^2). Substitution gives
$$A_i p_i^\alpha(1-p_i)=K,\quad A_i=g_i^{2\gamma/(\gamma+1)},\quad \alpha=(\gamma-1)/(\gamma+1),\quad \sum p_i=1.$$
Conversely any positive solution reconstructs an equilibrium: b_i=sqrt(p_i/(1-p_i)), R_ij=C0 b_i b_j/sum_{k<l in S} b_k b_l on S and zero otherwise. Indeed v_i proportional to sqrt(p_i(1-p_i)) satisfies the Perron equations, and the scalar relation implies u_i proportional to b_i. Thus the reduction is exact. At any such equilibrium the Perron choice is unique, so it is fixed for every admissible selection.
Finiteness
For gamma<1, each A_i p^alpha(1-p) decreases strictly from infinity to zero. Its inverse decreases continuously from one to zero as K increases from zero to infinity. The sum crosses one exactly once. There is one equilibrium per support of size at least two, hence 2^N-N-1 (16,777,191 for N=24). This counts equilibria, not attractors.
For gamma=1, p_i=1-K/g_i and K=(m-1)/sum 1/g_i. At most one solution per support, admissible exactly when all p_i>0.
For gamma>1, 0<alpha<1. Each scalar function has one nondegenerate maximum at p_c=alpha/(1+alpha). Choose one of two inverse branches for each i. On 0<K<Kmax=min_i max_p A_i p^alpha(1-p), the sum minus one is real analytic. Near zero lower branches are O(K^(1/alpha)); upper branches are 1-K/A_i+O(K^2). With zero or at least two upper branches the limiting sum differs from one. With exactly one upper branch the negative linear term dominates. Thus no zeros accumulate at zero and the analytic function is not identically zero. Near Kmax each branch attaining its maximum is analytic in sqrt(Kmax-K) by the nondegenerate analytic maximum; other branches are analytic in K. Accumulation there would force an identity, already excluded. Interior accumulation is also impossible by analyticity. Include the endpoint Kmax separately and deduplicate coinciding branches. Finitely many branches and supports imply finitely many equilibria for every fixed parameter set. No explicit total count for gamma>1 is asserted.
Global convergence theorem
For any admissible trajectory R_n, L(R_n) increases weakly to a finite limit, hence its increments tend to zero. For any accumulation subsequence R_nk -> R*, extract a further subsequence of chosen unit Perron vectors v_nk -> v*. Closedness of the Perron equations gives an admissible v* at R*. The update is continuous in the pair (R,v) on this compact domain. The limiting Lyapunov increment is zero, so strictness implies F_v*(R*)=R*. Every accumulation point is therefore a fixed point with simple Perron eigenvalue.
The same compact-pair argument proves ||R_(n+1)-R_n|| -> 0: otherwise a subsequence of steps bounded away from zero would converge to a nonstationary pair with zero Lyapunov increment. Since the accumulation set is contained in a finite set, the distance to that set tends to zero. Choose disjoint neighborhoods of the fixed points with positive pairwise separation. Eventually every state lies in their union and steps are shorter than the separation, so the trajectory stays in one neighborhood. Only one fixed point can be an accumulation point. Compactness then gives convergence to that point.
This proves individual global convergence for every initial state and every sequence of admissible Perron selections. It does not imply uniform convergence, global contraction, uniqueness of the limit across initial conditions, or convergence to a globally maximal Lyapunov value. Parameters must remain fixed along the trajectory.
Quotient and connections
For weight-preserving permutations, the correspondence descends to orbit classes and every projected trajectory converges to a fixed class. A chosen single-valued map descends only if it preserves orbit equivalence. A heteroclinic trajectory with distinct fixed endpoints must have strictly ordered Lyapunov values; therefore no directed heteroclinic cycles exist. Basin boundaries are not thereby classified. For a continuous single-valued map the boundary of an asymptotic basin is positively invariant by complete preimage invariance of the basin; a homeomorphism gives equality. For a continuous open quotient projection, the preimage of a boundary equals the boundary of its preimage. These statements do not assert global continuity of a Perron selection.

It does not certify exhaustive root enumeration, long-time convergence numerically, global separatrix regularity, or alternative algorithms.
